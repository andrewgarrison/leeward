# Leeward: Story Outline

## Tone

Modern day, grounded, and personal. The player should feel like they are the one stranded, in the spirit of *Cast Away*. Relics and wrecks from the past are welcome, but nothing feels medieval or magical. The island is sunlit and the player is capable; the sea is the dangerous part.

Progression borrows from Old School RuneScape: many trades, long level curves, gated areas, and interlocking skills. The feel does not.

## The shape of the world

The archipelago sits inside **the Leeward Current**, a ring of weather and magnetic anomaly that has pulled ships and planes off course for about a century. Compasses spin, radios die, and search planes fly past without seeing the islands. That is why nobody comes.

The map is a target. Your crash put you inside the ring and home is outside it.

- **Castaway chapter:** you sail outward to escape.
- **After the door:** you sail inward toward the Eye.

## The castaway arc

Six acts, one per build in the brainstorm. Each can be played on its own.

### Act 1: The Daze (Build 1, Morning)
You wake in the surf with a cut on your forehead and a seatbelt buckle in your fist. The beach is scattered with what the plane dropped: a split suitcase, a seat cushion, an orange life vest. Find the stream, start a fire with a lens from broken glasses, build a lean-to before dark. The fire is still burning when you log back in. No numbers yet.

### Act 2: The Camp (Build 2, Work)
Trades show up as habits before they are skills. Hunger and thirst are real threats. The turning point is the first morning you don't have to worry about water, when the island stops being a trap and becomes a place to live. The tide brings in one piece of wreckage every few real days.

### Act 3: The Raft (Build 3, The First Boat)
A log, vine, and tarp raft. The first attempt is meant to fail: the surf flips it, the cargo is lost, you wash back up, and Sail still goes up. This teaches the core promise: the sea takes your stuff, never your progress. The act ends past the reef, with a flare going up over the horizon.

### Act 4: The Island Opens Up (Build 4, A Full Island)
First Light is bigger than the beach: a lagoon, bird cliffs, a jungle interior with a 1943 lookout, a cave behind a waterfall. Areas unlock by trade level. The mystery wakes up: a child's drawing of a house with a blue door, a cracked phone that powers on for a few seconds. The relic shelf begins.

### Act 5: The Other Shores (Build 5, Another Shore)
The pilot's abandoned camps, a tally of days, notes. Each new island adds its own trade and piece of the story. Any island can become home. Crossings get serious: weather windows, provisions, hull types.

### Act 6: The Door (Build 6)
The Storm Wall crossing on a sloop built from several islands' materials, using a chart that is half yours and half the pilot's. A coast guard cutter picks you up just outside the wall and brings you into **Harborlight**. The relics have built a face, a street, and a blue door. The door opens. The house becomes home and the sea becomes a place you choose to go. The old islands and your camp are still out there.

### Daily pull, every act
The tide brings new wreckage, weather changes which crossings are safe, fish traps fill while you're away, and the night sky teaches Navigation one star at a time.

## Who is behind the door

Your past life is made of several people, and all of them are real. The relics decide whom you remember most strongly, and that person opens the door.

### Memory threads
| Thread | Example relics |
|---|---|
| Partner | Wedding ring on a broken chain, a voicemail on the cracked phone, a shared playlist on a waterlogged music player |
| Child | The blue-door drawing, a small sneaker, a stuffed animal that washes up months in |
| Parent | A handwritten recipe card, an old watch, a letter mailed before the flight |
| Sibling | A concert wristband, a photo-booth strip, a half-finished postcard |
| Dog | A chewed tennis ball, a collar tag, a jacket covered in dog hair |

### The shelf is the choice
The hut has limited display spots, expanded through Craft. You find more relics than you can show. Displayed relics are the memories you choose to keep; stored relics go in a chest and can be swapped back. Each displayed relic triggers a short memory flash (a voice, a room, a smell).

### The reveal
The night before the final crossing the shelf locks in and you dream the strongest thread clearly for the first time. The thread with the most weight on the shelf opens the door. Ties go to the relic found first.

### Everyone else is still out there
After the reunion, the other threads become reunion quests from town: a sibling in a coastal city, a dog taken in by a lighthouse keeper, a parent writing weekly letters to the harbor master. Every player eventually finds everyone, in their own order.

### Your role in town
Your strongest trade on arrival suggests your old job. High Cook: you ran a harbor café. High Sail: ferry captain. High Craft: boatbuilder. Tinkering: the town's radio and electronics repair person.

## Why the plane went down

This is the one mystery every player shares. The people waiting are personal; the crash is not.

The flight (working name: **Flight 417**, a small charter) entered the Current. The instruments died. The pilot (working name: **Captain Ren Hollis**) chose to ditch near the islands to give everyone a chance. The pilot survived and is always one island ahead of you.

| Act | Reveal |
|---|---|
| 1 | A calm voice on the intercom: "Hold on, I've got us." A hand-drawn arrow on the back of your life vest. |
| 2 | The pilot's flight bag washes up. Last readable logbook line: "Compass spinning. Taking her down near land." |
| 3 | A flare over the horizon on your first night past the reef. |
| 4 | A 1943 radio operator's diary in the lookout: the same spinning compass, wind from everywhere, rescue planes that flew past. This has happened before. |
| 5 | The pilot's camps, notes, stone cairns, carved arrows, and supply caches before hard crossings. Always an empty camp, always one island behind. |
| 6 | A sealed chart tube at the pilot's last camp: "This way out. I'm going the other way." The pilot is heading for the Eye. |
| After | The pilot arrives at Harborlight and asks you to crew a run at the Eye, the endgame voyage. |

The Current stays grounded: a scientific mystery a research team would want to solve, not a portal.

## The archipelago

### Castaway chapter: six islands, from the crash outward

| # | Island | Feel | Trade | Wreck | Pilot sign |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **First Light** | Warm, green, safe once earned | Forage, Cook, Craft, Sail | Your plane's debris, the 1943 lookout | The arrow on your life vest |
| 2 | **Tallyrock** | Windswept rocky islet, raft distance | Fishing | — | The pilot's first camp and tally marks |
| 3 | **Ember Isle** | Volcanic, black sand, hot springs | Salvage | A 1980s fishing trawler | A supply cache before the next crossing |
| 4 | **Gullhaven** | Tall sea cliffs, birds, rope bridges | Tinkering | A 1930s seaplane on a cliff ledge, with its own pilot's journal about the Current | Carved arrows along the cliffs |
| 5 | **Saltglass Shoals** | Turquoise atoll and lagoon | Diving | A grounded 2000s container ship (sneakers, solar lamps, rubber ducks) | A cairn on the outer reef |
| 6 | **Lantern Key** | Ruined lighthouse at the edge of the Current, Storm Wall on the horizon | Navigation | — | The pilot's last camp and the chart tube |

**The Storm Wall:** the hardest voyage of the chapter. On the other side is Harborlight.

### Hulls
| Hull | Opens |
|---|---|
| Log raft | Tallyrock |
| Dugout outrigger | Ember Isle, Gullhaven |
| Sail canoe | Saltglass Shoals, Lantern Key |
| Deep-keeled sloop | The Storm Wall |

Each hull is a large Craft project using materials from more than one island, so older islands stay useful.

### After the door: the Fogbound islands
The Current shifts with the seasons. Each content update brings a new island out of the fog, deeper in than before, each with its own wreck and a new trade or higher ceiling. Early ideas:
- A research station drowned in the 1960s
- An abandoned 2000s resort hotel: drained pools, a dusty gift shop, a ballroom full of seabirds
- A reef of sunken airplanes

At the center is **the Eye**, the endgame voyage with the pilot. It can wait years.

## Skills

Levels run 1 to 99. Reaching Harborlight takes roughly 50 to 60 in the key trades; maxing everything takes years. Levels are never lost.

**Ceilings:** each trade has a cap you can't train past until you find the right manual or tool. Finding them pulls you to new islands.

### Forage (First Light)
- 1: Coconuts, crabs, fallen fruit
- 15: Tide pools and seaweed; a basket to carry more
- 30: Jungle interior, cliff paths, bird eggs, wild taro (absorbs the old Climbing idea)
- 50: Medicinal plants that heal injuries from bad crossings
- Ceiling item: a dog-eared plant field guide from a carry-on

### Cook (First Light)
- 1: Charred fish on a stick; burning is common
- 20: Smoking rack; food keeps for crossings
- 40: Salting with sea salt; stews in a salvaged pot
- 60: Full meals that buff a crossing (stamina, slower thirst)
- Ceiling item: the parent thread's recipe card, or a galley cookbook from the trawler

### Craft (First Light)
- 1: Sharpened sticks, lean-to, woven palm mats
- 10: Log raft
- 25: Raised hut with a relic shelf
- 35: Dugout outrigger
- 55: Sail canoe
- 75: Deep-keeled sloop
- Ceiling item: a boatbuilding manual from the trawler

### Sail (First Light)
- 1: Paddle inside the reef
- 10: Cross the reef break in calm weather
- 30: Read swells; moderate wind
- 50: Night crossings; survive a squall
- 70: Attempt the Storm Wall
- Ceiling item: the pilot's notes on the Current's winds

### Fishing (Tallyrock)
- 1: Hand line from fishing line and a bent paperclip
- 20: Spearfishing on the reef
- 40: Woven fish traps that fill while you're away
- 60: Deep-water trolling from a moving boat
- Ceiling item: a tackle box from the container ship

### Salvage (Ember Isle)
- 1: Plane scraps: seat fabric, wire, plastic panels
- 20: Aluminum sheet for tools and a water still
- 40: The trawler: copper, rope, an engine block
- 60: Breaking into shipping containers
- Ceiling item: a multi-tool and hacksaw set sealed in the trawler's toolbox

### Tinkering (Gullhaven)
- 1: Fix a flashlight with salvaged batteries
- 15: Solar charger keeps the cracked phone alive a few minutes a day, unlocking photos and voicemails as relics
- 35: Hand-crank radio: static, then a news report about Flight 417
- 55: A working GPS unit
- 75: The seaplane's radio sends a signal out of the Current
- Ceiling item: a ham radio handbook from the 1943 lookout

### Diving (Saltglass Shoals)
- 1: Breath-hold dives in the shallow lagoon
- 25: Free-diving the reef wall
- 45: Salvaged mask and tank; the container ship's flooded hold
- 65: Deep salvage on the sunken parts of your own plane, where the best relics are
- Ceiling item: a dive computer from a container

### Navigation (Lantern Key)
- 1: Direction from the sun
- 20: Reading stars
- 40: Plotting a course with the pilot's partial chart
- 60: Navigating while the compass spins inside the Current
- 70: Plotting the Storm Wall crossing
- Ceiling items: the lighthouse keeper's tide tables, and the GPS from Tinkering

### How they interlock
- The sloop needs Craft, Salvage for metal fittings, and Tinkering for a working radio.
- The Storm Wall needs Sail and Navigation, plus Cook and Fishing for supplies.

### After the door
Harborlight's marina, hardware store, and library hold higher-tier manuals and tools. The Fogbound islands carry levels from 80 to 99 and add new trades over time (ideas: Engineering, Marine Biology, Photography).
